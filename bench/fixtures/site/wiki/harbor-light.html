Merrow Point Harbor Light

The Merrow Point Harbor Light is a disused lighthouse on the Merrow Point breakwater, at the entrance to Larkhaven harbour. It guided the herring fleet and the packet boats for almost a century, and is now a listed building run by the Larkhaven Maritime Trust.

History

After the loss of the schooner Alba on the Merrow Ledges in 1866, the harbour commissioners asked the engineer Thomas Ardley for a light on the new breakwater. A temporary lantern was hung from the harbour wall in 1869 while the tower was built of granite from the Carrow quarry.

The tower was finished in the autumn of 1873. The light was first lit on 3 March 1874 by its first keeper, Edda Marsh, who tended it for thirty-one years and kept the keeper's log that the Larkhaven Museum still holds.

A fog bell was added in 1881 and replaced by a diaphone in 1923. The light was electrified in 1937, automated in 1961 and put out of service in 1972, when the outer mole light took over.

Keepers

After Edda Marsh retired, her assistant Jonas Penhale became keeper in 1905. He was followed by Ruth Penhale, his daughter, and later by Aled Morgan, the last keeper before automation. Their cottages on the Quay Street side of the breakwater are now holiday lets.

Lens and light

The first lens was a fourth-order Fresnel lens made by the Ardley glassworks, lit by a paraffin lamp. It was replaced in 1902 by a larger lens with a clockwork drive that gave one white flash every five seconds.

Today

The Larkhaven Maritime Trust opens the tower on summer weekends. The lamp room has been restored, and a small display tells the story of the Merrow Ledges wrecks.
